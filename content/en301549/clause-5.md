# Clause 5 — Generic requirements

Clause 5 sets out baseline requirements that apply broadly across ICT
products and services, independent of whether they're delivered as a
website, an app, or a physical device. Later clauses (6–13) layer more
specific requirements on top, depending on what the product actually
does.

## What it covers

- **Closed functionality** — where a product's interface genuinely can't
  be made accessible through built-in means (for example, some
  single-purpose ticket machines), Clause 5 requires an alternative way
  to access the same functionality.
- **Biometrics** — where a product uses biometric authentication (e.g.
  fingerprint or face recognition) as the only way to operate it, an
  alternative method must be available for people who can't use that
  biometric method.
- **Privacy** — accessibility features (such as text-to-speech readback
  of a PIN) must not compromise a user's privacy or security any more
  than the standard, non-accessible method does.
- **Non-visual and visual access** — operable parts, status indicators
  and controls must be identifiable and usable both visually and
  non-visually, not relying on a single sense.
- **Colour and contrast** — colour must not be the only way information
  is conveyed, and text/background contrast must meet minimum ratios.
- **Operable parts** — controls must be reachable and operable without
  requiring fine motor control, simultaneous actions, or tight grasping
  and twisting.

## Who this applies to

Effectively every ICT product or service in scope of the Web
Accessibility Directive or the EAA picks up at least some Clause 5
requirements — it's the common baseline sitting underneath the
web/software/hardware-specific clauses.
