# Clause 8 — Hardware

Clause 8 covers the physical, tactile side of ICT accessibility —
relevant to any product with a physical control surface: kiosks, ATMs,
ticket machines, payment terminals, remote controls, and general-purpose
hardware like phones and laptops.

## What it covers

- **Standard connectors** — where a product has audio output, it should
  offer a standard connection point (e.g. a headphone jack) so people
  can use their own assistive listening or captioning hardware.
- **Operable parts** — physical controls must be reachable and operable
  without requiring fine motor control, tight grasping, or simultaneous
  multi-point actions.
- **Tactile and visual identification** — controls should be
  distinguishable by touch (e.g. a raised marker on the “5” key) as
  well as by sight, and not rely on colour alone.
- **Key repeat** — where a device has repeating keys, the repeat rate
  should be adjustable or able to be turned off, so users with limited
  dexterity don't trigger unintended repeated input.
- **Status indicators** — power/on-off and other device status should be
  perceivable both visually and non-visually (e.g. a tone, not just an
  LED).

## Who this applies to

Self-service terminals and kiosks, ATMs, ticket machines, payment
terminals, and general ICT hardware — this is the clause that applies
regardless of what software is running on the device.
