# EN 301 549 V4.1.1: what's changing and when

Every clause page on this site is written against **V3.2.1** - the
edition currently cited in the EU Official Journal, and so the version
with legal effect for EAA and Web Accessibility Directive presumption of
conformity. But a newer edition, **V4.1.1**, has already been published
by ETSI and is working its way toward taking over. This page covers what
it changes and when it's expected to matter.

## What's changing

V4.1.1's headline change is moving the web, non-web document and
software clauses (9, 10 and 11) from **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** to **WCAG 2.2
Level AA**, adding six new success criteria that V3.2.1 doesn't require:

- **2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (AA)** - a focused element can't be
  entirely hidden behind a sticky header, cookie banner or other overlay.
- **2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA)** - any drag interaction needs a
  single-pointer alternative (e.g. tap-to-select instead of drag-to-reorder).
- **2.5.8 Target Size (AA)** - pointer targets need a minimum 24×24 CSS
  pixel hit area, with limited exceptions.
- **3.2.6 Consistent Help (A)** - help mechanisms (contact details, chat,
  FAQ links) must appear in the same relative order across pages.
- **3.3.7 Redundant Entry (A)** - information a user already entered in a
  process shouldn't have to be re-entered, unless it's essential.
- **3.3.8 Accessible Authentication (AA)** - cognitive function tests
  (e.g. puzzles, memorisation) used for login can't be the only
  authentication option, without an accessible alternative.

Beyond WCAG 2.2, V4.1.1 also substantially reworks **Clause 6**,
widening it from "two-way voice communication" to real-time
bidirectional communication more broadly - including expanded real-time
text (RTT) requirements, interoperability between RTT implementations,
and a "Total Conversation" model where voice, text and video can be used
together in a single session. User-preference requirements are also
reworked, and the standard's EAA and Web Accessibility Directive annexes
have been updated to map against the revised clauses.

For clause-by-clause detail beyond this summary, see the standard
itself - linked below - or the [EN 301 549 overview](/overview) for how
V3.2.1's clause structure (which V4.1.1 keeps) fits together.

## When it takes effect

V4.1.1 does not yet have legal effect, and there's no confirmed date for
when it will. What's confirmed so far, from the published standard
itself:

- **Published:** 2 September 2026 (adopted by ETSI on 24 August 2026).
- **National standards bodies** must publish V4.1.1 as an identical
  national standard, or endorse it, by **31 May 2027**.
- **Conflicting national standards** - including national editions of
  V3.2.1 - must be withdrawn by **31 May 2028**. After that date, V4.1.1
  is the only version in force nationally.

The step that actually matters for legal effect - citation of V4.1.1 in
the EU Official Journal, which is what confers presumption of conformity
under the EAA and Web Accessibility Directive - has **no confirmed
date**. Some sources online cite specific dates (a recurring one is
"October 2026"; some vendor write-ups have suggested other months) but
none of these have a primary source behind them, and some appear to
conflate the OJ citation with the unrelated national-standards deadlines
above. Treat any specific date you see elsewhere as unconfirmed unless
it links back to the Official Journal itself.

Until V4.1.1 is cited, **V3.2.1 remains the version with current legal
effect** - see the [timeline](/timeline) for the full chronology and
sourcing on each date above.
