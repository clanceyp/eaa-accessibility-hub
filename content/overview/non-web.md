# EN 301 549 V3.2.1 - Non-web

Away from the browser, EN 301 549 still applies — to native mobile apps,
self-service terminals and kiosks, electronic documents, and physical
ICT hardware. These requirements sit alongside [Clause 9](/overview/clause-9)
(Web) and are just as often in scope of the **European Accessibility Act**.

## Native software and mobile apps

**[Clause 11](/overview/clause-11)** covers software, including native mobile and desktop
applications. It mirrors the WCAG success criteria conceptually (via the
“WCAG2ICT” mapping) but restates them in software terms — covering things
like:

- Platform accessibility APIs being correctly exposed (so screen readers
  like VoiceOver and TalkBack can interpret the UI)
- Keyboard/switch-equivalent operability in non-web software
- Closed functionality (software with no accessible interface at all,
  e.g. some ticket machines) still needing alternative access

## Self-service terminals, kiosks and hardware

**Clauses 5–8** and **[13](/overview/clause-13)** address physical hardware and terminals:

- **[Clause 5](/overview/clause-5)** — Generic requirements: closed functionality, biometrics,
  privacy, non-visual/visual access to controls, colour and contrast,
  audio output, operable parts, tactile indication of speech mode
- **[Clause 8](/overview/clause-8)** — Hardware: physical access, standard connectors, layout
  and tactile identification, key repeat, colour coding
- **[Clause 13](/overview/clause-13)** — ICT providing relay or emergency services

Common examples: ATMs, ticket vending machines, self-check-in kiosks at
airports and stations, and payment terminals.

## Documents

**[Clause 10](/overview/clause-10)** covers non-web documents — PDF, Word, ePub, spreadsheets
and similar formats — distributed as part of a product or service. It
applies WCAG-equivalent requirements adapted for document formats:
structure, tagging, reading order, alt text, and form fields.

## Support services and documentation

**[Clause 12](/overview/clause-12)** requires that documentation and support services (help
desks, user guides) are themselves accessible — available in accessible
formats and covering accessibility features of the product.
